# What is MyMind?

Most tools help you *record* thinking. MyMind is built to help you *do* it.

A note app gives you documents. A mind map gives you a hierarchy of labels.
Neither can tell you much about what you wrote — they store the words and
leave the reasoning in your head.

MyMind stores the reasoning too. Cards have **types**, so the app knows a
decision from an assumption. Connections carry **meaning**, so a line
between two cards says *why* they are related, not merely *that* they are.

That difference is the whole point. Once the structure is real data rather
than a picture, the map can answer questions: what does this decision
actually rest on? Which options have nothing supporting them? If this
assumption turns out to be wrong, what falls over?

You can use MyMind as a plain canvas of markdown notes, and it works fine
that way. But the moment you start typing your cards and annotating your
connections, it becomes something else — a map of an argument you can
interrogate, revisit, and check against what actually happened.
